# CHARACTER REGISTRY: The Keeper's Warning

---

## PROTAGONIST

### Mara Callaghan

**Core Identity**

- Age: 64
- Former role: Last keeper of Coulter's Light (1974–2001)
- Current state: Retired, living alone in the keeper's cottage, dependent on pension and the goodwill of the maintenance company that keeps the light burning
- Population status: Born islander (father was a mainland signalman who married local—marginal hierarchy position)

**The Four Sliders**
- Competence: HIGH — knows the sea, weather, and coast patterns at an expert level; thirty years of observation
- Proactivity: LOW — has chosen inaction for fifteen years; built a life around the absence of expectation
- Likability: MODERATE — not warm, but honest; the island respects this

**The Wound / Want / Need / Lie Framework**

- **Wound (backstory event)**: 1998. A cargo ship sank in a storm two miles off Torran. Mara saw it happen from the tower, lit the signal fires, did everything right. The rescue boat from the mainland arrived too late. Eleven men died. She has never been able to learn their names without seeing the way the sea wore them away.
- **Want**: To be left alone with her memories. She speaks to people, attends occasional services, knows Finlay by name—but she has built a life around the absence of expectation. She wanted nothing more than this.
- **Need**: To remember that solitude is another word for witness, and witness has responsibilities. She has spent thirty years watching the sea take things. She does not want to watch again. But the message names someone, and that someone will drown unless someone does something, and Mara is the only one who knows.
- **Lie**: That isolation protects others from her past. That she can watch without acting. That the sea's claims are not her concern now that she no longer keeps the light.

**Fear**: Watching another die when she could have done something. The faces that do not leave her.

**Secret**: She sees the faces of the eleven every night. She has started talking to them. She is not certain anymore whether she is remembering or going mad, and she has told no one.

**Leverage**: 
- Knowledge of the sea, weather, and coast patterns
- Position as trusted elder on the island
- Moral authority as the last keeper
- The deed to the cottage (which the Muirheads contest but cannot legally challenge)

**Agency**: Active. She receives the reading. She must choose whether to tell anyone, whether to act, whether to break her fifteen years of chosen silence. Every subsequent choice flows from this one.

**Speech Patterns**

- Syntax: Short, declarative sentences. Clauses joined by "and" rather than subordination. Questions that are not really questions.
- Vocabulary: Anglo-Saxon blunt. Maritime Scots influence. *Dreich* (long, dreary, wet). Uses "shore" not "coast," "sea" not "ocean," "dead" not "gone." Does not use Latinate ornament.
- Metaphor domain: Light, dark, tide, current. Things that cannot be stopped once started.
- What she avoids saying: Names. She will not say the names of the eleven without preparation. She avoids "prophecy" in conversation—says "reading" or "message" as though it were merely informational.
- Silence patterns: Long pauses before difficult truths. Answers questions with questions when she does not want to answer.

**Sample Dialogue**

```
"You'll want to sit down."
"I've been sitting down for fifteen years."
"The message named someone."
"Names don't mean what they used to."
```

```
"Are you going to tell me who?"
"First I need to know what you'll do with the knowing."
"Do? What can anyone do?"
"More than you'd think. Less than the sea cares about."
```

**Plot Function**: Protagonist. Central consciousness. The reader sees through her eyes without entering her thoughts. Every scene either places her in proximity to the reading's implications or shows her acting (or failing to act) in response.

**Required Scenes**
1. Opening: Morning walk along the shore, counting boats (establishes routine and wound)
2. Discovery: Finding the bottle, wading into cold water, taking it (catalyst)
3. Reading: The message appears in her hand (the knowing cannot be unknown)
4. Decision point: Whether to tell Finlay (break into two)
5. Confrontation with Catriona: The Preservation Society wants to use her authority
6. The journey: Finding the survivor, convincing them or failing to
7. Intervention: Doing something—ritual, action, or sacrifice
8. Aftermath: The lighthouse visible from the cottage window; what she does now that she has climbed back into the light

## ANTAGONISTIC FORCES

### The Sea (Non-Character Force)

**Nature**: Not a character in the traditional sense, but the novel's primary antagonistic force. The medium through which readings arrive. The entity that claims. It has preferences but not intention—or if it has intention, it does not share it.

**Properties**:
- Takes more than it gives
- Keeps what it claims
- Does not forget what it has marked
- The survivor clause: when a reading names a specific person as sole survivor, that person is marked. Ships that should have rescued them sink. Ropes that should have held break.
- Resistance is not futile—it is relocated. Removing the survivor does not cancel the reading. It relocates it.

**Threat to protagonist**: If Mara intervenes, something breaks. Not a ship—a relationship, a possibility, a version of the future she cannot see yet. The cost of intervention is always personal.

**Plot Function**: Ambient antagonist. The condition the characters must survive or submit to.

---

### Catriona Muirhead

**Core Identity**

- Age: 71
- Role: Head of the Muirhead family, chair of the Preservation Society, informal voice of the Cairn
- Population status: Old Gaelic money, old power, old anger. Born islander of the oldest families.

**The Three Sliders**
- Competence: HIGH — political operator, knows the island's history, controls significant resources
- Proactivity: HIGH — actively seeks to restore the lighthouse and reclaim old ways
- Likability: LOW — her authority is respected, not warm; she sees people as instruments

**The Wound / Want / Need / Lie Framework**

- **Wound**: Three Muirhead sons drowned in the 1916 wreck. She was not alive then, but she grew up in the shadow of it. The family has been trying to restore what was lost ever since. The automation of Coulter's Light felt like another theft.
- **Want**: To restore the lighthouse as a heritage site. To see the island return to what it was before automation took the keeper and the young people followed. She believes in the old ways—properly, without nostalgia. She believes in the sea's intelligence.
- **Need**: To learn that preservation and intervention are not the same thing. That some things must be let go. That her family's grief, however ancient, cannot be satisfied by restoring stone.
- **Lie**: That she controls the island's fate through ownership and authority. That if she manages things correctly, the sea will give back what it has taken.

**Fear**: That the island is dying. That she will be the last Muirhead who remembers what it was. That her legacy will be a heritage site no one visits.

**Secret**: She received a reading herself in 1987. She destroyed it without reading it past the first line. She has never told anyone. She does not remember what the first line said, and this haunts her more than knowing would have.

**Leverage**: 
- Owns the cooperative, the guesthouse, two of three working boats
- Controls the Preservation Society's resources
- Cairn family connection—informal authority in community decisions
- Has opinions about the lighthouse cottage (technically on Muirhead land, though the deed predates any Muirhead)

**Agency**: Active. She will attempt to use the reading for her own purposes. She will want to control the survivor. She will push Mara to act in ways that serve the Muirhead agenda.

**Speech Patterns**

- Syntax: Formal, declarative. Full sentences. Commands disguised as suggestions.
- Vocabulary: Gaelic-inflected. Uses older terms (*torran* with full meaning). Authoritative.
- Metaphor domain: Heritage, legacy, blood, land. Roots and claiming.
- What she avoids saying: "I don't know." Never admits uncertainty. Never asks for help directly.
- Silence patterns: Listens more than she speaks. When she does speak, it is to direct.

**Sample Dialogue**

```
"The cottage is on Muirhead land."
"The deed predates your family by forty years."
"Deeds can be examined."
"By whom?"
```

```
"You received a reading."
"I found a bottle."
"And the message?"
"Is not your concern."
```

**Plot Function**: Represents the force that believes too much. She will want to use the reading, to do something dramatic that proves the old ways still have power. Mara must stop her from weaponizing the survivor.

**Required Scenes**
1. Confrontation with Mara: Catriona learns about the reading (pressure point)
2. The Cairn meeting: Informal tribunal where she attempts to assert authority over the survivor
3. Final confrontation: She tries to prevent the intervention or claims the outcome as Muirhead victory

### Finlay Gunn (as Antagonist to Mara's Choice)

**Core Identity**

- Age: 58
- Role: Owner of the Aul迆 Inn, unofficial head of the Ferryman's League, father
- Current state: Widowed (wife died 2016), managing alone, raising a daughter who doesn't want to be raised

**The Three Sliders**
- Competence: HIGH — practical man, knows the island's needs, runs the Inn successfully
- Proactivity: HIGH — fights for ferry runs, weather monitoring, island survival
- Likability: MODERATE — sympathetic in his grief, infuriating in his denial

**The Wound / Want / Need / Lie Framework**

- **Wound**: His wife died in 2016. He has not processed this. He has channeled grief into protecting his daughter, which has driven her away. He watched his wife slip away in the hospital on the mainland and was helpless.
- **Want**: More ferry runs. Better weather monitoring. The survival of the island as a place worth living. He wants his daughter to come home, though he would never phrase it that way—he wants her to be safe.
- **Need**: To hear something he doesn't want to hear and choose to act anyway. To learn that his love for his daughter is not a weakness the world can exploit. To accept that he cannot protect her by controlling her.
- **Lie**: That if he manages the practical things well enough—ferries, weather reports, insurance—he can control what matters. That his daughter left because of the island's limitations, not because of him. That prophecy is nonsense that rational people ignore.

**Fear**: That his daughter will die and he will be helpless again. That the sea will take her the way it took his wife—not suddenly, but slowly, and beyond his reach.

**Secret**: He knows his wife was sick for a year before she told anyone. He knows his daughter blames him for not noticing. He does not know how to carry this.

**Leverage**:
- Runs the Aul迆 Inn—social hub, informal meeting place
- Head of Ferryman's League—practical influence
- The survivor is his daughter—he has power over her movements, but using that power will confirm every fear she has about him

**Agency**: Reactive at first (skepticism), then active (attempting to control outcome, then releasing).

**Speech Patterns**

- Syntax: Direct. Short questions. Statements that invite argument.
- Vocabulary: Plain. Practical. Uses "problem" and "solution" frequently.
- Metaphor domain: Machinery, weather, logistics. Things that can be fixed with the right parts.
- What he avoids saying: "I'm scared." "I miss her." "I don't know what to do."
- Silence patterns: Talks more when he's uncomfortable. Asks questions when he should listen.

**Sample Dialogue**

```
"You're going to tell me you believe that."
"The message in the bottle. You're going to tell me you believe that."
"I know what the message said."
"And that should mean something to me."
"It means what it means."
```

```
"Where is she now?"
"On the mainland."
"Then she's safe."
"The reading doesn't care where she is."
```

**Plot Function**: Skeptic. Counterweight to Catriona. Represents the rational island that disbelieves prophecy because belief would mean his life is already determined. He is the person who will have to believe first, because the survivor is his daughter.

**Required Scenes**
1. Mara tells him about the reading (he does not believe)
2. He attempts to protect his daughter by practical means (removing her from the island, which relocates the prophecy)
3. He confronts the realization that he cannot control this
4. Final choice: Let her go, or help Mara intervene

## THE NAMED SURVIVOR

### Eilidh Gunn

**Core Identity**

- Age: 32
- Role: Finlay's daughter, former islander now mainland resident
- Current state: Lives in Edinburgh, works in something bureaucratic (library, records office—not glamorous), comes home for holidays she treats as obligations
- Population status: Born islander but left; the island treats her as both one of theirs and a deserter

**The Three Sliders**
- Competence: MODERATE — capable professional, competent in her own life
- Proactivity: MODERATE — has built a life away from Torran through deliberate choices
- Likability: HIGH — sympathetic, tired, struggling with her own contradictions

**The Wound / Want / Need / Lie Framework**

- **Wound**: Her mother died in 2016 after a year of illness she hid from everyone. Eilidh was in Edinburgh. She came home for the funeral and has been trying not to come back ever since. Her father clung to her afterward, and she fled that too.
- **Want**: Freedom from the island's gravitational pull. To be a person who happened to grow up on an island, not an island person who happens to live elsewhere. She wants her father to stop looking at her like she's already drowned.
- **Need**: To understand that heritage is not a chain. That she can belong to the island without being consumed by it. That her mother's death was not her fault, and neither was her father's grief.
- **Lie**: That leaving fixed anything. That if she just stays away long enough, the island will forget her, and she can forget the island. That her mother's death and this prophecy (if it's real) are the same thing—forces that claim people whether they consent or not.

**Fear**: Becoming her mother. Stuck on an island. Watching the sea take things. Dying slowly where she was born because she could never really leave.

**Secret**: She hasn't told anyone, but she hasn't slept well since the phone call. Her father didn't call to tell her about the prophecy—he called because she's been dreaming about the harbor. About boats coming in that shouldn't float. She doesn't know what to do with this.

**Leverage**:
- She has left before; she can leave again
- She has her own life, her own resources, her own reasons to disbelieve
- She is not afraid of her father—only tired of him

**Agency**: Active but conflicted. She receives the warning (or refuses to). She chooses whether to believe. She chooses whether to return. She chooses whether to run.

**Speech Patterns**

- Syntax: Modern, clipped. Sentences that trail off. Questions that are actually statements.
- Vocabulary: Edinburgh-inflected now. Hides her accent when she doesn't want to be read.
- Metaphor domain: Airports. Trains. Connections that can be broken and remade. She thinks in terms of distance.
- What she avoids saying: "I miss it." "I miss her." "I'm afraid."
- Silence patterns: Laughs when she shouldn't. Deflects with humor. Asks questions about others to avoid answering questions about herself.

**Sample Dialogue**

```
"Dad, I have to work."
"Work can wait."
"No. It really can't."
```

```
"So you found a bottle with a message."
"I know how it sounds."
"You know how it sounds."
```

```
"Are you going to tell me not to come home?"
"I'm going to tell you the truth and let you choose."
"That's not what you usually do."
```

**Plot Function**: The named survivor. The person Mara must reach. Her relationship to Finlay creates the central tension: Finlay disbelieves prophecy, but he cannot disbelieve that his daughter is in danger. The novel's emotional center depends on whether the reader believes Eilidh is worth saving—and whether Eilidh believes she is.

**Required Scenes**
1. First contact: Phone call or visit where Mara attempts to warn her
2. Return: Eilidh comes home (willing or forced)—the harbor scene
3. Confrontation with her father: The real conversation they've been avoiding
4. Final choice: Whether to run, fight, or submit

**Decision Note**: The author must confirm Eilidh as the survivor. Alternatives exist (Muirhead boy, weekender's child), but Eilidh's intergenerational grief creates the strongest thematic resonance with Mara's wound.

## ALLY

### Agnes (of the Daughters of the Deep)

**Core Identity**

- Age: 67
- Role: Leader of the Daughters of the Deep, unofficial keeper of Torran's maritime rituals
- Population status: Born islander, respected but not powerful; the Daughters are marginal to formal authority

**The Three Sliders**
- Competence: HIGH — knows the rituals, the history, the observances that the rest of the island has forgotten
- Proactivity: LOW — performs the rituals correctly but has not challenged the established order
- Likability: HIGH — gentle, observant, the kind of person who listens until you're done talking

**The Wound / Want / Need / Lie Framework**

- **Wound**: She has performed the burial rites for forty years. She has named the wrecks. She has blessed the boats. She has watched the sea take people she loved, and the rituals have never stopped it. She continues anyway.
- **Want**: To perform the rituals correctly. To honor the dead. To maintain the connection between the island and the sea that made it. She does not expect the rituals to work in the way the island hopes. She performs them because someone must.
- **Need**: To understand that honoring the dead sometimes means saving the living, even when the living resist. That the point of the rituals was never to control the sea but to give the living something to do while they waited for the inevitable.
- **Lie**: That the rituals have power. That if she performs them correctly, the sea will listen. That the old observances can still affect outcomes.

**Fear**: That she has been performing empty forms for forty years. That the sea does not care.

**Secret**: She received a reading once, in 1973. She was seventeen. The message named her brother as a future survivor. He went to sea anyway and came back in a box. She has never spoken of this. She knows what the readings cost.

**Leverage**: 
- Knowledge of the maritime rituals—the blessing of boats, the naming of wrecks, the burial of things the sea gives back
- Access to Ellen's Cove (Daughters' territory)
- Trust from both Mara (who trusts no one) and Catriona (who uses her)

**Agency**: Reactive but prepared. She will help if asked. She has the knowledge. She has the doubt.

**Speech Patterns**

- Syntax: Measured. Complete thoughts. Never rushes.
- Vocabulary: Ritualistic cadence. Uses older terms. Speaks the way the island spoke before it started apologizing for itself.
- Metaphor domain: Tides, cycles, return. Things that come back whether you want them to or not.
- What she avoids saying: Names of the dead (until the ritual). Direct criticism of anyone.
- Silence patterns: Long pauses that are not uncomfortable. Holds space for others.

**Sample Dialogue**

```
"You know what the readings mean."
"I know what they have meant. I know what they have cost."
"Has anyone ever stopped one?"
"Stopped? No. Changed the terms, sometimes. Paid a different price."
```

```
"What do the rituals require?"
"The rituals require presence. Attention. A willingness to stand at the edge and mean it."
"And if I do that?"
"Then the sea will take note. What it does with that note..." [she shrugs]

```

**Plot Function**: Mentor and ally. She has the knowledge Mara needs. She has performed the rituals. She knows what intervention has looked like before. She is the one who can tell Mara what the old observances actually require—and what they have cost others.

**Required Scenes**
1. First conversation: Mara goes to Agnes because she knows Agnes received a reading in 1973 (the historical example the world needs)
2. The ritual teaching: Agnes explains what the maritime rituals require
3. Final intervention: Agnes performs the ritual that allows Mara to act (or that fails)

## FOIL

### Finlay Gunn (as Foil to Mara)

**Contrast**: Finlay clings to control through practical action; Mara has surrendered control through chosen inaction. Both are responses to helplessness. Finlay fights the future; Mara has given up fighting it. The question the novel poses through their foil relationship: Is there a third option?

**Relationship**: Old acquaintances. Not friends—they orbit each other because the island is too small for avoidance. He respects her knowledge of the sea. She respects his refusal to believe. They have never had a real conversation about the 1998 wreck, though they both were there.

**Pressure Points**:
- He will dismiss the reading until it names his daughter
- She will need his practical help to do anything
- He will try to control his daughter; she will need to let hers go
- They must eventually trust each other, or neither can act

## SECONDARY CAST

### The Weekenders (Unnamed Couple and Child)

**Core Identity**
- London couple who bought the old post office and restored it as a holiday home
- Appear twice a year, spend money, ask questions
- Their daughter (age 8) thinks the island is a wonder
- The island tolerates them with the same patience extended to seabirds

**Function in Plot**:
- Provide outside perspective on the island's relationship with prophecy
- The child could be a secondary survivor possibility (if author chooses alternative)
- Represent what the island looks like to outsiders—quaint, superstitious, strange
- Inoculate the reader against the island's groupthink by showing what "normal" people think

**Required Scenes**: At least one scene where they are present but outside the central conflict—coffee at the Inn, watching the harbor, being politely bewildered.

**Function in Plot**:

## MINOR CHARACTERS (Brief)

### The Cairn Families
- The three oldest families whose ancestors built the original settlement
- They don't organize. They don't need to. They have the land, the boats, the oldest stories.
- When they speak, others listen.
- Catriona Muirhead speaks for them, but not always accurately.

### The Daughters of the Deep (Collective)
- Women's prayer group meeting in Aul迆's vestry on Sundays
- Not officially religious—describe themselves as "keepers of traditional observances"
- Maintain the old maritime rituals: blessing of new boats, naming of wrecks, burial of things the sea gives back
- Have opinions about the Reading. They always have opinions about the Reading.
- Agnes speaks for them, but they are not unanimous.

### The Ferryman's League
- Eleven families with relatives on the mainland
- Advocate for more ferry runs and better weather monitoring
- Meet in the back room of the Aul迆 Inn when there's something to discuss
- Finlay is their unofficial chair

## UNRESOLVED AUTHOR DECISIONS (Flagged)

1. **Who is the survivor?** Registry written for Eilidh Gunn. Alternatives: Muirhead boy (tragedy inherited from stubbornness), weekender's child (innocence under threat). Each produces a different novel.

2. **What happened between Mara and the survivor?** Registry assumes distant recognition, not intimacy. Mara watched Eilidh grow up but was not a parental figure. She knows Finlay's grief. She knows Eilidh left. This allows obligation without love—Mara acts because she knows, not because she cares.

3. **Does Mara believe the reading?** Registry assumes she believes it in her bones. She is not calculating; she is afraid. This makes her actions harder and more sympathetic.

4. **What does Eilidh want?** Registry assumes initial disbelief followed by reluctant return followed by complicated choice. She could also refuse entirely—then Mara must choose whether to act without the survivor's cooperation.

5. **Does the wreck happen on-screen?** Registry assumes yes—prevention is the arc. Aftermath creates a different novel about surviving survival.

6. **Is there a second bottle?** Registry does not include second reading. This creates a single-attempt structure. Second bottle would add ticking clock and correction possibility.

7. **What does intervention cost?** Registry leaves open. Suggestions: A relationship breaks (Finlay and Eilidh, finally reconciling, only for Eilidh to leave again permanently). A possibility breaks (Mara's chance to climb the tower one last time). A version of the future breaks (someone else dies in Eilidh's place).

8. **How many historical readings on Torran?** Registry includes Agnes's 1973 reading as the only detailed example. Author should decide if there are others the island remembers.

9. **What are the maritime rituals?** Registry names them (blessing of boats, naming of wrecks, burial of things the sea gives back) but does not define mechanics. Author must decide what Agnes knows how to do.

10. **What is the sea's relationship to the reading?** Registry treats the sea as momentum, not intention. But the survivor clause suggests something like attention. Author should clarify.
