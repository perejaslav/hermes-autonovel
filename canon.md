## Geography

### Torran Island
- Granite outcrop roughly four miles long, two miles wide, off the northwest coast of Scotland
- Population: 347 at last census (down from 512 in 1971)
- Tapers to a hook of land in the east where the lighthouse stands on a promontory called the Keeper's Thumb
- Harbor village of Aul迆 lies in a natural cove to the west
- Interior is moorland, cut by three streams that run brackish in late summer

### The Keeper's Cottage
- Stone walls two feet thick, whitewashed inside, slate roof
- Two rooms: main living space with peat fire, sleeping loft accessible by ladder
- Sixty yards below the lighthouse tower
- Smells of salt, lamp oil, and rosemary on the windowsill
- Technically on Muirhead land, though the deed predates any Muirhead

### Aul迆 Harbor
- Natural cove, protected from northwest weather by a lip of granite
- Water too deep and tide too unpredictable for a dock; boats tie to mooring buoys
- Single street runs parallel to shore: the Inn, post office (now weekenders' holiday home), cooperative building, empty harbor master's office (vacant since 2011)

### Ellen's Cove
- Small, sheltered cove named for Ellen Coulter, who drowned here in 1901
- Considered unlucky; sea doesn't fight here, it waits
- Ground slopes gently into water waist-deep thirty feet out, then drops
- The Daughters gather here for observances

### The Lighthouse Tower (Coulter's Light)
- Built 1887, named for the engineer who designed it
- Sixty-two feet of granite and iron
- Modern light housing inside an original lens
- Automated in 1974, decommissioned from active service in 2001
- Off-limits except to company technicians; key is with maintenance company in Inverness

### The Cairn
- Pre-settlement burial mound, older than the island's name
- Untouched except by grass growth and occasional lightning strike
- Muirheads claim it's on their land, but no one has tested this
- Where the island goes to remember what it is; where the Cairn families hold informal tribunals

## Timeline

- **1847**: First permanent settlement on Torran (fisher families from Outer Hebrides)
- **1887**: Coulter's Light built
- **1901**: Ellen Coulter drowned in the cove now named for her
- **1916**: German U-boat sank British freighter two miles off Torran; island rescued eleven survivors
- **1973**: Agnes received a reading naming her brother as future survivor; he drowned
- **1974**: Coulter's Light automated; three families left that year; Muirheads petitioned against automation (petition failed)
- **1987**: Catriona Muirhead received a reading; she destroyed it without reading past the first line
- **1998**: Last major wreck in local waters (cargo ship lost in storm); eleven men died; bodies never returned
- **2001**: Lighthouse decommissioned from active service; Mara Callaghan withdrawn as last keeper
- **2011**: Harbor master's office became vacant
- **2016**: Finlay's wife died after a year of illness she hid from everyone
- **Story present**: Three weeks between reading discovery and predicted wreck

## Magic System Rules (The Reading / The Coasting)

### Core Mechanics
- Readings arrive in glass bottles; cannot be summoned, stolen, or bought
- Trigger unknown; correlations exist with high storms, specific tidal patterns, anniversary of mass wrecks
- Message is always written in the hand of the finder, but words arrive from outside
- If finder cannot write, message does not form
- If finder writes in multiple scripts, message appears in language their body defaults to under stress
- Readings tend toward maritime disaster: storms, wreck, drowning
- Sometimes name who survives, what they carry, how many bodies the sea keeps
- Rarely: name of a survivor who should not have survived

### The Survivor Clause (HARD RULE)
- When a reading names a specific person as sole survivor, that person is marked
- Ships that should have rescued them sink
- Ropes that should have held break
- The sea does not forget what it has claimed

### Limits and Costs (HARD RULES)
- Cannot be intercepted; passes through all barriers
- Once message has formed, only destruction of glass stops it—but by then the finder already knows
- Single reading per finder: a person who writes a reading cannot receive another; their hand belongs to the sea already
- No false readings; every reading in the Registry has come true

### Failure Cases
- Refusal to deliver: if named survivor is told and does not believe, prophecy still holds; survivor may survive wreck then die elsewhere or walk into sea months later
- Intervention by force: attempting to remove named survivor from island does not cancel the reading; it relocates it; ship still sinks, survivor still arrives somehow, drawn back by a current with no chart
- Second bottle: very rarely, a second bottle arrives after the first; happened four times in recorded history; second reading always corrects the first

### Social Consequences
- Registry clerks track readings and are generally disliked; a clerk's visit means someone received a message
- People who receive readings and survive often gain strange authority in island communities
- Islands Provisional Government has no law regarding prophecy
- Insurance adjusters have argued readings constitute foreknowledge and contributory negligence (never held)

## Character Facts

### Mara Callaghan
- Age: 64
- Last keeper of Coulter's Light (1974–2001)
- Father was a signalman from mainland who married local; marginal hierarchy position
- Lives alone in keeper's cottage, dependent on pension and maintenance company's goodwill
- Walks the shore each morning, counts boats she can see from tower height
- Wounds: 1998 wreck; eleven men died; she has their faces, never learned their names without seeing the sea wear them away
- Secret: She talks to the eleven every night; she is not certain whether she is remembering or going mad
- Smokes: Rosemary on the windowsill for memory
- Highly competent with sea, weather, coast patterns; chose inaction for fifteen years; not warm but honest

### Finlay Gunn
- Age: 58
- Owner of Aul迆 Inn; unofficial head of Ferryman's League
- Widowed (wife died 2016); has never processed this grief
- Has daughter Eilidh in Edinburgh; she treats visits as obligations
- His care is expressed through control; after his wife died, he clung to Eilidh and drove her away
- Does not believe in prophecy because belief would mean his life is already determined
- Fear: losing daughter the way he lost wife (slowly, beyond his reach)
- Secret: He knew his wife was sick for a year before she told anyone; knows Eilidh blames him for not noticing

### Catriona Muirhead
- Age: 71
- Head of Muirhead family; chair of Preservation Society; informal voice of the Cairn
- Old Gaelic money, old power, old anger
- Owns the cooperative, the guesthouse, two of three working boats
- Wants to restore lighthouse as heritage site; believes in old ways without nostalgia
- Claims cottage is on Muirhead land (claim never tested; deed predates Muirheads)
- Wounds: Three Muirhead sons drowned in 1916 wreck; family has been trying to restore what was lost ever since
- Secret: Received a reading herself in 1987; destroyed it without reading past the first line; never told anyone

### Agnes
- Age: 67
- Leader of Daughters of the Deep; unofficial keeper of Torran's maritime rituals
- Has performed burial rites for forty years; watched the sea take people she loved
- Wounds: 1973 reading named her brother as future survivor; he went to sea anyway and came back in a box; she has never spoken of this
- She knows what readings cost

### Eilidh Gunn
- Age: 32
- Finlay's daughter; former islander now mainland resident
- Lives in Edinburgh; works in records office (bureaucratic, not glamorous)
- Comes home for holidays she treats as obligations
- Hides her accent when she doesn't want to be read
- Wound: Her mother died in 2016 after a year of illness she hid from everyone; Eilidh was in Edinburgh
- Secret: Has been dreaming about the harbor—boats coming in that shouldn't float—since the phone call from her father
- She knew her mother was sick for a year; never told her father; this drives their distance
- Wants: Freedom from the island's gravitational pull
- Fear: Becoming her mother; stuck on an island; watching the sea take things

### Dr. Macallister
- Representative of the maintenance company that keeps Coulter's Light burning
- Appears quarterly for inspections
- Not unsympathetic but bound by company policy

### Weekenders (Unnamed Couple and Child)
- London couple who bought old post office and restored it as holiday home
- Appear twice a year; spend money; ask questions
- Daughter, age 8, thinks the island is a wonder

## Political / Factional

### Islands Provisional Government (IPG)
- Governs from a mainland town
- Torran sends one elected councillor on rotation
- No law regarding prophecy
- Licensed premises must close by nine (ferry leaves at 9:15)

### The Muirheads
- Oldest family, old Gaelic money predating croft acts
- Own the cooperative, the guesthouse, two of three working boats
- Have opinions about the lighthouse cottage
- Speak for the Cairn, but not always accurately

### The Ferryman's League
- Eleven families with relatives on mainland
- Advocate for more ferry runs and better weather monitoring
- Meet in back room of Aul迆 Inn when there's something to discuss
- Finlay is unofficial chair

### The Preservation Society
- Muirhead-adjacent
- Want to restore lighthouse as heritage site
- Catriona is chair

### The Daughters of the Deep
- Women's prayer group meeting in Aul迆's vestry on Sundays
- Describe themselves as "keepers of traditional observances"
- Maintain maritime rituals: blessing of new boats, naming of wrecks, burial of things the sea gives back
- Have opinions about the Reading; always have opinions about the Reading

### The Cairn
- Three oldest families whose ancestors built original Torran settlement
- Don't organize; don't need to; have the land, the boats, the oldest stories
- When they speak, others listen

## Cultural

### Maritime Rituals (performed by the Daughters)
- Blessing of new boats: salt, fire, words in Gaelic
- Naming of wrecks: speaking the names of the dead so they can rest
- Burial of things: giving the sea what it wants so it takes less
- The rituals don't stop the sea; they give the living something to do while they wait

### The Registry of Tidal Readings
- Maintained by scholars in the capital
- Cross-references predictions across centuries
- Data incomplete by design; most people who receive a reading destroy the message rather than file it

### Economy
- Fishing: herring and crab
- Aul迆 fleet is six boats; three working, three waiting for parts
- Wool and tweed cooperative (rough-spun goods sold through single Edinburgh shop)
- Ecological tourism (rugged, unspoiled)
- One supply ferry per week from mainland; if ferry doesn't come, island lives on canned goods for a month

## Open Questions (Blockers for Drafting)

1. **What are the specific maritime rituals?** The outline names them (blessing of boats, naming of wrecks, burial of things) but mechanics are undefined. Must establish what Agnes knows how to do before Ch 8 can be drafted.

2. **What is the sea's relationship to the reading?** The world bible treats it as momentum, not intention—but the survivor clause suggests something like attention. Recommendation: the sea has preference but not will; it takes more than it gives; it keeps what it claims; it responds to ritual but does not negotiate.

3. **How many historical readings have happened on Torran?** Agnes's 1973 reading is the only detailed example. Does the island remember others? Recommendation: at least one other is known (maybe Catriona's 1987 reading can serve this purpose), giving the community a framework for what readings mean.

4. **What does "intervention" look like in practice?** The world bible states that removal attempts relocate the prophecy but doesn't specify what successful intervention (other than the second bottle correction) might accomplish. The ritual cost mechanism in Ch 22-23 needs grounding in established precedent.

5. **What is the specific price Eilidh pays?** The outline establishes she loses "something she loves" but doesn't specify whether this is concrete (her relationship with Finlay, her ability to return, a person) or abstract (her sense of belonging). The distinction affects Ch 26's emotional weight.
